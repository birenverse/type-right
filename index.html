A beginner-friendly guide to touch typing — with the tools and steps to get you there.

Before You Start

These four rules matter more than everything else combined. Get these right and the rest follows.

👀

Don't Look at the Keyboard

Keep your eyes on the screen. Your speed will drop at first — that's part of learning.

🎯

Accuracy Before Speed

Fast + sloppy = bad habits. A slower, accurate typist is building a much better foundation.

🖐️

Use the Correct Fingers

Every key has an assigned finger. It feels awkward at first. That awkwardness is temporary.

🔄

Practice Regularly

Touch typing is a motor skill. Reading about it won't help. Your fingers need repetition.

The Learning Path

Follow these steps in order. Each tool has a specific purpose in your development.

1
Learn

TypingClub

typingclub.com

Your starting point. TypingClub teaches the keyboard progressively — individual keys, then combinations, then words, then sentences. Start from the beginning and don't skip ahead.

During lessons: Use the instructed finger. Don't look down. Slow down when you make mistakes. Repeat lessons that don't feel comfortable yet. Your goal isn't impressive WPM — it's teaching your fingers where the keys are.
Open TypingClub →
2
Train

Keybr

keybr.com

Once you know the keyboard layout, Keybr strengthens your individual key movements. It generates artificial text combinations to train muscle memory rather than word recognition.

🤔 "This isn't even English — what am I typing?"

That's intentional. When you type a familiar word like "the", your brain recognizes the whole pattern. You're not really thinking T → H → E separately. That's useful for real typing, but it hides weaknesses.

Keybr strips away word familiarity so your fingers do the actual work. Think of the generated text as a sequence of keyboard movements to practice, not words to understand.

Keybr also adapts to your weaknesses — characters you're slow with appear more often. Don't assume repeated letters mean something is broken.

How to use it: Don't worry about whether the text makes sense. Don't memorize it. Use the correct fingers. Don't race — let accuracy lead. Expect it to feel slower than normal typing. That's expected, not a bad sign.
Open Keybr →
3
Practice

Monkeytype

monkeytype.com

With a reasonable foundation, Monkeytype becomes your general practice tool. It gives you real English words and lets you develop speed, accuracy, rhythm, and consistency.

Start simple: Use normal English-word mode. Focus on typing accurately without looking down. Don't worry about every feature — just type.

4
Test

10FastFingers

10fastfingers.com

Use this to measure your progress, not as your entire training program. A test measures performance — it doesn't necessarily fix the reason you're slow.

Use the test to answer: "How well am I typing now?" Then go back to the appropriate tool to work on what you found.

5
Apply

Real-World Typing

The real goal isn't a high test score — it's effortless everyday typing. Emails, messages, documents, code. Real typing adds capitalization, punctuation, numbers, and unpredictable words.

You don't "graduate" from any tool. Move between them depending on what you need: Weak letter → Keybr. General practice → Monkeytype. Measuring progress → 10FastFingers. Forgot proper technique → TypingClub.

Understanding WPM & Accuracy

WPM (Words Per Minute) measures your speed. Accuracy measures how clean that speed is. Both matter.

✓ Better
40 WPM
98% accuracy
Fewer mistakes = cleaner typing
✗ Worse
50 WPM
80% accuracy
More mistakes = sloppy habits

Speed tells you how fast you're going. Accuracy tells you how cleanly you're getting there.

Why does my WPM keep changing?

That's normal. Results depend on which words appear, how familiar they are, your concentration, fatigue, and typing rhythm. A single test doesn't tell you much — look at your performance over many sessions.

Getting Faster

Speed isn't about forcing your fingers to move harder. It's about eliminating what slows you down.

Tip 1

Stop Looking Down

You can't build touch-typing habits while depending on your eyes. Make the screen your only visual reference.

Tip 2

Improve Accuracy

Making many mistakes? Slow down. Your current speed may be beyond what your fingers can reliably handle.

Tip 3

Eliminate Hesitation

Notice where you pause. Are certain letters slow? Particular combinations uncomfortable? These are your weaknesses to train.

Tip 4

Strengthen Weak Keys

Use Keybr to identify and practice the specific keys that are holding you back. Targeted practice beats random tests.

Tip 5

Develop Rhythm

Good typing feels smooth, not frantic. type type type type type beats type → stop → type → stop. Smoothness comes before speed.

When You Feel Stuck

Almost everyone hits these bumps. Here's what to do.

"I keep looking at the keyboard."

Slow down and force yourself to keep your eyes on the screen. It will feel worse before it feels better. That's normal.

"I'm extremely slow."

That's expected when learning. Don't respond by abandoning proper finger placement. Give your fingers time to learn the movements.

"I keep using the wrong finger."

Don't ignore it. Slow down and consciously use the intended finger until the correct movement becomes habitual.

"I'm stuck at the same WPM."

Don't just take hundreds more speed tests. Look for the cause: weak characters? Frequent mistakes? Hesitation? Identify the bottleneck and train that specific weakness.

"My hands feel tense."

Don't type through pain. Reduce your speed, relax your fingers, and avoid pressing keys harder than necessary. Make sure your posture is comfortable.
